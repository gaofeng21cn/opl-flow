# Human Archive And Active Projection

Use this contract for Ledger enrollment, human-facing fact updates, and historical
copy retirement. Reuse the existing Supervisor, task owners, and private Instance.
Do not introduce another scheduler, database, executor, or giant parent task.

## Owners And Goal Granularity

- Beads/Dolt owns execution facts, dependencies, current owner, and remaining work.
- Obsidian Markdown owns the complete human archive. Bases indexes these same
  notes into all-work, unfinished/frozen, and historical-outcome views.
- Linear is an optional projection of goals selected for current progression,
  decision, or acceptance. It remains a source of authorized human intent.
- Original sessions, GitHub, and artifact owners supply business evidence.
  Fleet owns admission, capacity, and execution attempts; no display replaces it.

One independently deliverable or decidable goal has one stable identity. A
session, retry, small fix, or version update normally updates that goal's progress.
Split goals only when their deliverable, owner, decision, or acceptance can be
managed independently. Use domain and period as navigation; do not keep hundreds
of closed children under a never-ending umbrella merely to preserve history.
Do not infer hours or effort from session/card/test counts. Describe actual scope,
outputs, unresolved work, and evidence so the human can assess direction and effort.

Each note explains why the work matters, what was verified, what remains, the
acceptance boundary, responsible owner, source identity, and evidence links.
Store execution, source, candidate, installed, runtime, and domain acceptance as
separate facts when relevant. Completed work can retain explicit quality debt.
A frozen goal remains unfinished and is never dispatched without renewed intent.

## Archive And Synchronization

Resolve the owner's existing Vault and configured archive location; keep private
paths and accounts in local Instance configuration. Never publish archive contents
or copy the Vault through Fleet. Use the authorized Obsidian/file interface, read
before writing, preserve manual additions and stable source identifiers, and stop
on conflicting concurrent changes instead of overwriting them. Bases properties
are task-control fields; they do not change unrelated knowledge-note conventions.

Import the full source body, comments with stable IDs, relationships, and available
source metadata; retain raw source snapshots when exporting historical copies.
Binary attachments need their own verified backup: a link is not a binary backup.
State the imported scope and missing content. A local write does not prove cloud
synchronization or continued access to an externally hosted attachment.

After a fact change, update the corresponding archive note and selected Linear
projection, then read back both. Verify notes through their real storage interface
and verify Bases queries/counts separately. A mapping, export receipt, or successful
write alone does not prove readable full coverage. If a required read/write fails,
retain the pending event and affected cursor; do not claim full alignment. Preserve
unhandled authorized comments until their delivery/answer readback chain closes.

Historical records need no Linear card. An unprojected unfinished goal keeps its
owner, remaining work, and a reason or pending review for projection selection.
Idle workbench roots and distant event-triggered duties can stay archive-only;
keep currently actionable work and explicitly frozen visible responsibilities
in the selected projection. This never closes the underlying responsibility.

## Retiring Linear History And Reporting Quota

First verify preservation. Then distinguish these actions in both receipts and
human reports: saving in Obsidian, leaving a project, platform archival, recoverable
deletion, and permanent deletion. They are not interchangeable. Done status,
project removal, a shorter active view, or an export does not prove quota recovery.
Read actual workspace usage and the current plan/archival rules from Linear; never
hard-code a plan limit or claim capacity from project membership counts.

Do not delete historical copies merely because a migration or optimization was
requested. Obtain explicit deletion authorization for a concrete reviewed scope.
Retain unfinished/frozen cards, unhandled human intent, unbacked attachments, and
necessary relationships; inspect parent/child deletion effects before acting.
A clearly authorized recoverable delete uses the platform's supported recovery
flow, records the exact IDs and recovery conditions, and reads back actual usage.
Permanent deletion is a separate irreversible boundary. A failed/unknown mutation
requires read-only reconciliation before retry; do not fabricate freed capacity.

After retirement, preserve source identity and removal disposition in the archive
and existing Supervisor mapping so later coverage checks cannot recreate the old
cards. Report archive coverage, unfinished responsibilities, active projections,
and actual workspace quota as different counts. Complete a finite stage/project
when its real deliverables are accepted; do not close ongoing work to chase quota.
